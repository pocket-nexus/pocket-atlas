# Repository instructions

- Pocket Atlas owns the places (`web/src/places/<id>`), the cooker, the `.place` pack format and the PS Vita / PSP renderers. PocketJS (pinned in `vendor/pocketjs`) owns the Vita dev host, the wired debug transport (`tools/vita-dev.ts`, `tools/vita-usb.ts`), VPK packaging (`tools/vita-package.ts`) and the pinned PSP toolchain resolver.
- Do not edit the submodule to fix application behavior. Send reusable changes to PocketJS, then update the pinned revision here.
- Use Conventional Commits for commits and pull requests (`type(scope): summary`). Publish validated changes as a Draft PR.
- Do not commit a build or a pack, and do not attach one to a GitHub release: the packages for each device are published on Pocket Studio (https://studio.pocket.nexus), where its members download them.
- `bun tools/release.ts` builds the packages Pocket Studio takes (README "Releases") by calling the device tools' own commands: a change to how a target is built goes in that target's tool. It computes each Vita program's name from `vita/shaders` as `build` in `vita/src/shaders.rs` does; a change to that hash or to `expand` changes `programHash` in `tools/release.ts` in the same commit. A place whose export takes more than its authoring definition to fit a device (Sangubashi's handheld geometry) is a row of `EXPORT_OPTIONS` there. The Android package is signed by a key file outside Git; `ANDROID_SIGNER` in `tools/release.ts` is its certificate's SHA-256, and a key never enters the repository. `dist/release/` and `.pocket-studio.json` stay out of Git.
- Keep captures, logs, cooked packs, USB-share contents and build receipts in the ignored `.pocket-build/`. Put reproducible commands, results and limits in the PR description instead of committing them.
- Commit an image only when something consumes it (LiveArea art under `vita/assets`, a test fixture).
- Separate evidence kinds when reporting: host build, on-device compile, device GPU timing (`bun tools/atlas.ts profile`), and what a person saw on the screen.
- PSP: use `tools/atlas-psp.ts` and exactly one `usbhostfs_pc` owner. PSP `gpuWaitMs` is the GE wait remaining after CPU submission, not serialized GPU timing. Keep the `PLPS` payload separate from the Vita format; measure every camera, report missed frame budgets, and leave a normal release running after validation.
- iPod touch 4: use `tools/atlas-ipod.ts` (`ipod/README.md`). Its pack is the PICA table with GLES texels: `pica.rs` cooks both, and a change there must leave the 3DS pack byte-identical unless the 3DS is meant to change. Frame time follows submitted triangles (about 0.2 ms per thousand), not pixels; keep the EAGL layer untransformed with no view over it; measure every authored shot with `shots` and read the status after the window, not during it.
- Browser tab (`wgpu/`): the atlas screen drawn with wgpu over WebGPU. `web/` is the three.js reference, not this target. The globe is `ipod/src/globe.c`'s in WGSL (`wgpu/src/globe.rs`, `shaders/globe.wgsl`): a change to a program there goes to both in one commit. A place is the PS Vita's renderer on wgpu (`wgpu/src/places/`, `shaders/place/`) from the PS Vita's pack, named in `place::RENDERER`: a change to a program in `vita/shaders` or to a pass in `vita/src/frame.rs` goes to its WGSL and its pass there too, and `wgpu/README.md` lists what differs on purpose (no far tier, the sun map's slope bias and four samples a texel, the emission of people inside a shop): do not bring one back to the console's without reading it. Positions and texture coordinates stay bound as `sint16`: naga 25's Metal backend swaps the components of `snorm16`. Do not fake a place or a loading screen that never ends.
- Browser tab: what is not this game's is PocketJS's browser kernel, `vendor/pocketjs/devices/web/pocket-web-wgpu` (the device and its screens, the overlay pass, ranges of a pack, the title card first and the frame loop, the interface's guest in PocketJS's realm, a handheld's controls and screens on the page and their stylesheet). This repository holds no copy of it: a change to one of those parts goes to PocketJS, then the pin moves. Every read of a file goes through its `Source`.
- Browser tab: the interface there is the bundle `tools/atlas-ui.ts` compiles for each device, run as it is, turned 30 times a second with `simHz` 30: a difference between the tab and a device is the page's or the shell's (`wgpu/page/main.js`, `wgpu/src/app.rs`), not a branch in `ui/`. `crates/pocket-atlas-interface` is used without a feature; a change there for the tab must leave the device builds as they were. The page's copy has no em dashes, the choice of device is plain text, and no device's case is drawn.
- Browser tab: a change reports `cargo test` in `wgpu/` (the globe, the flow around a place against a stub renderer, the guest's turns) and `bun tools/wgpu.ts check` (Chrome over WebGPU: the tab's frame beside this machine's, every device's atlas screen driven by keys and pointer, a place entered through the interface and left, every place's held view beside this machine's, what a frame and a redraw of the interface cost; it needs the packs under `.pocket-build/places`), and `check --dist` when the page's files or the reads change. The deployable directory (`bun tools/wgpu.ts dist`) is for a host that limits a file to 32 MiB and a deployment to 4 000 files and 1 GiB, and keeps `play/` and `runtime/` at the top for itself; it stays under `.pocket-build/` and no tool here uploads it.
- Redmi 1S (Android 4.3, Adreno 305, GLES 3.0): use `tools/atlas-android.ts` (`android/README.md`). Its pack is the PICA table with ETC2 texels, cooked relief and the sun's shadow camera: `pica.rs` cooks it with `adreno.rs`, and a change there must leave the 3DS and iPod packs byte-identical. Keep every triangle of the scene in the window's pass and take multisampling from the window's EGL config: a pass whose binning the driver gives up costs 25 – 40 ×, and that was seen on textures and multisampled textures. Keep a fragment program at fetches and one `mix` (arithmetic is what this GPU is short of), and draw anything that may `discard` in two stages, depth then colour at equal depth. `eglSwapBuffers` returns when a frame is queued, not when it is shown: judge a shot by the compositor's record (`shown` in `shots`, `cadence`) and its mean interval, not by a count of late frames, and keep the 30 fps pacing on the display driver's refreshes (`vertical_sync`, `latched`, `slot_after` in `android/src/main.c`). MIUI asks on the screen at every `adb install`: use `native` in the edit loop, and `install --release` for what stays on the phone.
- The Pocket3D title card plays first at every launch, on the Vita, the PSP, the 3DS, the Redmi 1S, in the reference and in the browser tab, before the renderer starts and before the interface boots: `pocket3d_title::vita::play()` in `vita/src/main.rs` (before `graphics::init_with_pool`), `title()` in `psp/src/main.rs` (before `renderer::init`), `pocket3d_title_play()` in `n3ds/src/main.c` (after `gfxInitDefault`, before `C3D_Init`), `title()` in `android/src/main.c` (first in `run()`, before `graphics()`; it draws PocketJS's frames through one texture), `playTitle()` in `web/src/main.ts`, `titleCard(playTitle)` in `wgpu/page/main.js`. It comes from PocketJS (`vendor/pocketjs/engine/pocket3d/crates/pocket3d-title`), and the Pocket3D License makes showing it a condition of distributing a product built on Pocket3D: do not skip, shorten, recolour or redraw it here, and do not draw the mark in a renderer, a place or the interface (`ui/`). A development build on the Vita skips it with `{"title": false}` in `host0:atlas/boot.json`, one on Android with `native --no-title` (an Android release build has no switch); `?shot` and `?export` runs of the reference skip it, so exports, previews and captures keep their timing. The iPod touch app has no card: `pocket3d-title` has no drawer for it.
- The app icon on the PSP, the Vita, the 3DS, the iPod touch and Android is the Pocket3D icon from `vendor/pocketjs/engine/pocket3d/icon/`, read there by each build: `xmb_icon_png` in `psp/Psp.toml` and `POCKET3D_ICON.psp` in `tools/atlas-psp.ts`, `icon: POCKET3D_ICON.vita` in `tools/atlas.ts`, `ICON` and `SMALL_ICON` in `n3ds/Makefile`, `POCKET3D_ICON.ios` and `.ios2x` in `tools/atlas-ipod.ts`, `POCKET3D_ICON_ANDROID` in `tools/atlas-android.ts` (one file a density, copied into the staged `res/drawable-<density>/`, never into `android/`). This repository holds no icon file: do not add, draw, capture, resize or recolour one, and do not copy PocketJS's files into Git. A capture of the game goes to `psp/assets/pic1.png` or the LiveArea pictures under `vita/assets/sce_sys/livearea/contents/`, never to an icon. `tools/app-icon.test.ts` checks both the tracked files and the wiring. Procedure: `vendor/pocketjs/skills/pocket3d-brand/SKILL.md`.
- Shader programs are compiled on the device by SceShaccCg; `bun tools/atlas.ts lint` catches syntax and type errors on the host but not SceShaccCg's overload ambiguities (mixing `half` and `float` in `lerp`/`smoothstep`), so cast explicitly.
- Upload uniform arrays at the program's declared length: `sceGxmSetUniformDataF` writes past a shorter parameter into the rest of the uniform buffer (it crashed the device).
- After the app dies on the device, restart `bun tools/atlas.ts serve`: the kernel driver reconnects, but the old host session stays dead and Devkit cannot report status.
- Everything flat on a handheld's screen is the interface, one PocketJS app (`ui/`), with a presentation per form of device (pad, two screens, touch). A renderer draws the scene, hosts the app as a guest and speaks `ui/app/protocol.ts` with it; it draws no text or controls of its own, and what a button or a touch means is decided in `ui/`. Give a new verb to every presentation, run `bun test ui/test`, and look at `bun ui/test/preview.ts <device>` before a device.
- The guest costs frame time: a renderer gives it a turn only when a button, a touch, the state or its picture changed (else about once a second), and timers in `ui/` follow the wall clock (`ui/app/clock.ts`), not the framework's frame count. Measure a place with and without it (`bun tools/atlas-3ds.ts profile --no-interface`).
- A place's rendering work goes into the shared renderer, cooker and web materials, keyed by the kind of place (night street, daytime slope, interior, …), not into code that only one place can use.
- Places built from film or anime stills recreate the real location only: no characters from the source. Take geometry and details from photographs and pilgrimage records of the real spot.
- To make, rebuild, polish or review a place, follow the `pocket-atlas-place` skill (`.claude/skills/pocket-atlas-place/`): research, scene, fidelity pass, device loop, budgets and the traps the first four places hit.
